# Black Atlas: sourcing and reuse policy

Effective October 15, 2026.

## Historical claims

Every milestone must identify the evidence for its date, summary, and context. Prefer original records, archival catalogs, museums, universities, and specialist historical institutions. Distinguish a primary record from a later account. An institution hosting a document does not necessarily endorse the document’s claims.

Write independent summaries from the evidence. Do not copy articles, closely rewrite their sentences, reproduce their distinctive structure, or treat a reference article as a database to import. Verify disputed claims against more than one source, describe uncertainty, and avoid false precision for estimates and long movements. Record the source title, publisher, direct URL, source type, and actual access date. Add an author, publication date, page or section locator when available and needed to find the evidence.

The initial milestone selection used [HISTORY’s Black History Milestones](https://www.history.com/articles/black-history-milestones) for topic discovery. Its text, quotations, and photographs were not imported. Each entry links to the evidence used to write it. The collection is introductory and selective, not a complete chronology. Its newest entry is from 2022; an atlas year extending to the present does not mean every later year has been researched.

## Text and quotations

A citation is not permission to reproduce protected expression. Historical facts and their expressive presentation are different: see the [U.S. Copyright Office’s explanation of what copyright protects](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html).

Current milestone entries contain original summaries and no direct quotations. Before adding a quotation, record its exact source and locator, speaker or author, date, context, and documented reuse basis. Link to a protected speech, poem, song, or article when permission or another defensible basis is absent. Do not assume that attribution, educational intent, age alone, or a short length automatically authorizes copying. The Copyright Office explains that [fair use has no automatic word-count allowance](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html). Case-specific legal review may be needed if relying on fair use.

## Photographs and other media

For every published asset, keep the creator, institution, original item URL, caption, date depicted, license or rights basis, and any required credit. Document the reviewer and date when conducting a new rights review. Record changes such as cropping and format conversion. Keep license text or permission evidence with the project where applicable. Different rights can apply to an object, photograph, scan, transcription, and accompanying web text.

Use original project assets, demonstrably public-domain material, appropriately licensed material with its conditions fulfilled, or material covered by recorded permission. A government or museum website can host third-party copyrighted work. Verify the specific item; do not infer permission from its host. A Library of Congress “no known restrictions” statement is an advisory, not a guarantee of public-domain status. Preserve the institution’s wording and obtain additional review when the basis is uncertain.

Unverified material must stay outside `public/`, production builds, screenshots, and distributed source packages. The supplied March on Washington photograph with an unidentified photographer has been withheld from the public archive and retained privately for possible identification. Do not republish older ZIPs or builds as if they passed this policy.

No new historical images are included in the milestone collection. Existing map and font assets retain their own attribution and licenses. Generated geographic illustrations must be identified as illustrations, never presented as historical evidence.

## Corrections and expansion

Before publishing an addition, check that every factual assertion is supported, the prose is independently written, any quoted wording is exact and authorized, and every media asset has documented reuse evidence. Automated checks can detect absent metadata and unregistered archive files; they cannot determine historical truth, plagiarism, or legal clearance.

When correcting an entry, update the text, its supporting references, and the review date together. Keep stable milestone IDs so existing links continue to work. Retain a record of substantive changes in version control. Source access dates describe the review at that time; they are not a claim that outside pages never change.
